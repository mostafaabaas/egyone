Guide 1 · Choosing an agency

How to choose a marketing agency in Egypt: the questions and the red flags

The right agency is not picked by the prettiest pitch deck — it is picked by specific questions with written answers. Before you sign with any agency, these are the questions that separate general talk from real work:

Ask before you sign

  • Who actually does the work? A fixed in-house team, or freelancers assembled per project? And ask to speak with the account team itself, not only the salesperson.
  • Who have you worked for before me? Real client names and published work you can check yourself — and work in your category, or one close to it, is a strong sign.
  • Who owns the content? The videos and photos produced: where may you use them, for how long, and are the raw files included? This point is written into the agreement from the start — it should never be discovered after delivery.
  • What does reporting look like? Ask for a sample report from previous work: what you will see, how often, and which numbers you will judge success by together.
  • What is inside the price — and what is outside it? Production, account management, ad spend, usage rights — each item separately, in writing. A price with no written scope grows in the middle of the project.
  • What if the first month disappoints me? What is the adjustment or exit mechanism, and is there a small pilot start before any long commitment?

Red flags — walk away calmly

  • Guaranteed numbers. "We will get you X followers" or "X views, guaranteed" — nobody can guarantee a social result, and anyone who does is selling you words.
  • A price with no written scope, or pressure to sign before you understand exactly what you will receive.
  • A stock-looking portfolio with no client names and no live work you can open and verify.
  • "We do everything" with no visible specialty — an agency with no focus will experiment on you.
  • Pressure for a long contract immediately, and refusal of any small trial start.

Guide 2 · UGC in Egypt

UGC for Egyptian brands: what actually works in this market

UGC works in Egypt when the video talks the way people talk — in their dialect, around the price-and-value questions inside the buyer's head, from creators the customer knows or resembles. These are the rules we see repeat across almost every category:

Dialect is not a detail — it is the message

A translated script, or stiff formal Arabic, is instantly recognisable as an ad that arrived from outside the market. The videos that work are said in natural Egyptian Arabic, in the customer's own words for their problem — and even for a global brand, the Egyptian version must be written from zero, not translated.

The creator who looks like your customer beats the celebrity

For everyday products, a small or mid-size creator whose audience knows them and trusts their taste is more believable than a big name who passes over dozens of products. Ask yourself: who would your customer believe, trying this product on camera?

The "how much?" question is not a flaw — it is the content

Every purchase decision in Egypt passes through a price-and-value comparison. A good video answers it naturally: what the product solves, what it saves, and why it is worth its price — without turning into a price announcement or invented claims.

The ending lives on WhatsApp

A large share of buying in Egypt is completed inside a WhatsApp conversation: a question, a confirmation, an order. So the video must close with one clear next step — exactly what the viewer should do now — not fade into nothing.

Timing changes everything

Ramadan, summer, back-to-school, the discount seasons — each one changes what people buy and what they watch. A good UGC campaign is built around its season, not repeated unchanged all year.

Guide 3 · Market geography

Marketing in Cairo vs Alexandria vs the Delta: the practical differences

Egypt is not one market with one voice. Greater Cairo, Alexandria and the Delta cities each have their own rhythm and way of buying, and a campaign that addresses everyone with the same cut weakens everywhere. Practically, the difference shows up in three things: the creators, the filming locations, and the seasonal calendar.

Greater Cairo: speed and variety

The biggest market, the fastest trends, and the fiercest competition for attention. And even inside Greater Cairo, the audiences of New Cairo and 6th of October differ from those of the older districts in taste, purchasing power and the content they respond to. Most production and events concentrate here, so content has to be faster and clearer to get through the noise.

Alexandria: local identity and a whole summer season

Alexandria has a strong local pride, and its audience responds to whoever speaks to it as a city in its own right, not as an annex of Cairo. Summer is its biggest season — the sea and the holiday crowds change the rhythm of the whole city and what content works in it — and an Alexandrian creator carries real weight with their audience.

The Delta: trust travels person to person

In cities like Mansoura and Tanta, buying moves through family, community and word of mouth: one trusted recommendation spreads fast — and a bad experience spreads faster. Work here is won with practical proof — a product honestly tried on camera — and with a creator from the same city, who is stronger for a local brand than any famous Cairo face.

The practical takeaway

The same product, but different creator lists per region, filming locations the audience recognises, and a seasonal calendar that is not one-size-fits-all. When you plan a campaign, first decide who it is for and where — then build the content on the answer.
