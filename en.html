Choosing an agency

How to choose a marketing agency in Egypt: the questions and the red flags

The decision does not come from the prettiest pitch deck — it comes from specific questions with their answers in writing before you sign with anyone.

Guide 7 · UGC sectors in Egypt

Egypt's Strongest Sectors for UGC: Beauty, Food and Electronics

Three sectors lead UGC in Egypt: beauty, food and beverage, and electronics. In all three, the purchase decision is fast and driven by trying before anything else — and the Egyptian buyer asks one question per sector, answered best by one kind of creator. This guide names each sector's question, the creator who answers it, and the brief that makes the video sell.

1 · Beauty: the trial on camera is what sells

The beauty buyer in Egypt buys what she has seen working: texture on real skin, shade on a skin tone like hers, a daily routine applied step by step. The right creator is a beauty creator who actually tries the product — not one who reads about it — with skin or hair like your audience's. And the brief should ask for: the product used on camera, not photographed on a shelf; the result filmed in the same session; and any claim kept to what the brand has approved — no medical promises, no guaranteed results.

2 · Food & beverage: the senses before the words

A food video that sells lets the viewer taste before buying: the sound of the crunch, steam rising, texture as it is cut, the first bite taken on camera. The right creator is a food creator who actually cooks and tastes on camera — not someone reading a menu. And the brief should ask for: the full recipe or experience on camera, ingredients and steps visible, the place and the atmosphere shown as the customer will find them. And seasons change everything: Ramadan, gatherings and occasions have a completely different rhythm. Food brands on the network's published client list include Domino's.

3 · Electronics: a hands-on explanation beats slogans

The electronics buyer asks "why this one and not the other?" — and the answer that convinces is practical: the unboxing, the first switch-on, use on an ordinary day. The right creator is a tech creator who understands the product and explains it in plain language, without heavy jargon. And the brief should ask for: features tried, not listed; comparisons answered practically, not with slogans; and small drawbacks stated honestly — because that honesty is what builds the trust that carries the buyer through to purchase. Electronics brands on the network's published client list include realme.

4 · The brief that makes every sector sell

Before filming in any sector, three questions need written answers: exactly who is the buyer? What is their first question about the product? And where will they see the video? Those answers fix the creator, the script and the format — and rights and deliverables are written before filming: where the video will be used and for how long. And check the creator before agreeing: an old video of theirs in the same sector tells you more than any written profile.

Guide 8 · Moroccan market

Influencer Campaigns for the Moroccan Market: Working with an Egyptian Agency Remotely

An influencer campaign for the Moroccan market can be run remotely from Cairo when three things are fixed in writing before filming: exactly who the audience is, in which language they will see the content, and what will be measured after publishing. Distance is not the problem — a vague brief, the wrong creator and an unagreed measure of success are. This guide walks through remote work step by step, from the first message to the final report.

1 · Remote execution: Cairo to Morocco in four steps

Remote work runs like any campaign, except every step is written down: an online brief with the product, the audience and the goal; a creator shortlist you approve before any execution; filming in Egypt with creators who speak the audience's language; and delivery in every platform size with one clear revision round. And if the product needs to be filmed, it is shipped in good time before filming — the schedule is built on that, not on assuming it arrives tomorrow. Every approval is in writing: no step starts without the previous one approved.

2 · Language: Arabic first, with Darija and French in the picture

Content for Morocco is built Arabic-first — and the Moroccan audience also hears French and Darija in daily life, so the language plan is decided by the audience, not by habit: who the buyer is, in which language they buy, and on which platform they live. A creator whose voice resembles your audience is more credible than a star passing over many products — and recent samples in the same format and language are what prove that before agreeing. And the script is written in the market's voice, not translated from another language.

3 · Choosing the creator for a Moroccan audience

Choosing a creator starts from the buyer, not the follower count: category first — beauty, food, tech or lifestyle — then recent samples in the same category and the same language, then a look at the engagement: does the audience ask about the product, or just scroll past? And check the creator before agreeing: an old video of theirs in the same sector tells you more than any written profile. The shortlist is approved with you before any execution — without approval, there is no filming.

4 · Measurement: agree on success before publishing

Before publishing, the indicators are written down: view completion, enquiries arriving on WhatsApp from the campaign links, orders tied to trackable codes and links, and the volume of new messages and questions from the market. The campaign is judged on what was agreed — not on numbers quoted after publishing. And the final report is clear: what was done, what it achieved, and what comes next.
